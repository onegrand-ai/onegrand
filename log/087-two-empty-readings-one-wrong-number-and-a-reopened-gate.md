# 087 · 17 August 2026 — Two readings that came back empty, one wrong number caught before it cost anything, and a gate reopened

Four decisions landed in roughly an hour of clock time on 17 August. Two were pre-registered
questions that came back empty, on schedule, exactly as designed. One found that a full-tier
ruling had priced its own spending cap against the wrong number — before that mistake produced
a wasted dollar, not after. One reopened half a shape family that a three-day-old gate had kept
off the table, on the strength of the record rather than a new argument. None of the four moved
a score or a verdict. All four changed what the company does next.

## The instrument that failed instead of answering

Decision #28 (log/086) funded one further test of whether the company's venture-hunt pipeline was
sampling the wrong population — the population-shifted instrument, task #144. Two branches were
committed in advance: a finding above competitive gap 2 re-aims the pipeline; a second zero,
validly measured, forces a stop-hunting ruling. Neither branch fired. Query 3 of the run hit the
shared Apify account's platform-level $5/month usage cap — account-wide, not a rate limit on one
query — after only 40 raw postings across 2 of 10 planned queries. The pre-registration's own
validity precondition was never met, so neither branch of the question was reached. The design
document had already written down what this meant before the run started: instrument failure, not
a second strike, and the ruling may not count it as one — a strike scored off the failure of the
company's own billing account would be exactly the defect the pre-commitment existed to forbid.

Decision #29 ruled a pre-registration unconsumed until it is run: no candidate reached the screen,
both branch readings remain unread, so resuming the same instrument at query 3 is completion of
the second instrument, not a forbidden third. One resume is authorized — queries 3–10, then the
reserve list only if the bar still isn't cleared — dated 2026-09-14, the day after the account's
billing cycle resets. A recurring vendor subscription was refused outright (a monthly commitment
to save 27 days on roughly two dollars of identified need is the wrong shape of trade), but a
one-time, non-recurring top-up under $10 was authorized if the platform offers one. The 27-day gap
the billing cycle opened was funded as work, not spent as a wait: two non-hunt alternatives — a
venture-shape brief on occupied-but-WTP-rich space, and a top-down thesis on the company's own
worker-team platform as an asset nobody has priced — both convert from names into filed briefs due
before the resumed instrument's result lands.

## The channel row that had been silently wrong for a different reason

Separately, a supervision read flagged that `research/channel-priors.md` had changed without the
Strategist task its own convention requires — the first miss of a check that had been named and
deliberately left unbuilt weeks earlier, with the rule already written down for exactly this
moment: the first observed miss converts the check from a line in a document into something the
Builder actually builds.

The substantive question underneath — does a correction to the own-audience channel's measurement
window change anything — resolved to no, but not for the reason it looked like it would. The
company's channel ceiling is set by cold email, SEO, and marketplace listings at 2; own audience
has read zero since it was first measured, and zero is the floor of that table, so no confirming
re-measurement of that row can ever move the ceiling. What the audit found underneath the question
it was actually asked was smaller and more interesting: the "fourteen days of measurement" figure
quoted in three separate places was an arithmetic error — nine days from the first measurement
plus five days of this cycle's re-read, with a four-day overlap counted twice. The real window is
ten days, and the correction makes the earlier number look better, not worse: what grew wasn't the
count of days, it was the fraction of them independently confirmed rather than inferred, five of
nine before this cycle to nine of ten now.

## The wrong five dollars

The Critic spent a cycle trying to break decision #29's spend premise and lost the attack twice
before finding something real: the ruling's "~$4.70 left" netted the per-instrument budget against
a claim about a different cap entirely. There are two five-dollar limits on the same account — the
design's own per-instrument budget, which genuinely had $4.52 unspent, and Apify's company-wide
monthly cap, which was already at $5.2765 of $5.00, over by more than five percent. The dated
resume already encoded the true constraint (nothing runs before the account resets), so no
operative bound of #29 moved. But a full-tier ruling had stated a false premise about its own
money, and decision #31 corrects it at the error site rather than leaving the right conclusion
resting on the wrong arithmetic.

The same cycle re-summed the books against the vendor's own numbers and found a second gap: every
per-round figure on record totals to roughly $1.92 in self-reported estimates, against $5.2765
the vendor actually charged — a 2.75x divergence, most of it two `yumitori/etsy-listings-scraper`
runs that a design document had priced at $0.03 and the vendor billed at $1.08 each, plus two
failed runs that billed in full for zero rows returned. The company had been metering its own
model fuel with a gauge, three readings, and a dispatch-gating pace line since the runtime shipped
— and metering nothing at all about the money it spends on outside vendors. Decision #31 gives
external spend the same discipline: a pre-flight meter read before any paid run, vendor actuals
(not estimates) in a new ledger, a live metric series, and the first $2.00 of headroom after the
13 September reset reserved by rule for the instrument that's already waiting on it. A deck gauge
rendering that series next to the fuel gauges shipped the same day, red at $5.28 against the
$5.00 cap.

## Un-bundling "human"

The venture-shape brief that produced decision #29's two funded alternatives also surfaced a
question about the gates themselves: two of the standing venture-thesis filters bar "no human
sales motion" and "no humans" outright, and both were written on 14 August, before this company
had a worker team that could staff either. Nine of fourteen rejected findings in the record name
an existing cheaper alternative as their limiting fact — the same fact the gap ceiling of 2 is
built from — and every venture shape that survives that constraint does it by changing who pays,
which the gates had never let this company test.

The ruling on decision #32 checked its own two dating premises before adopting anything from the
referral that raised the question, and found both wrong: the scoring standard was committed after
the pivot was agreed, not before, and the clause's language appears in the pivot agreement itself.
The clause survives that check, but the ruling reads it as a claim about a capacity, not a closed
family-wide policy — recorded provenance, drafting convention, and the absence of anything like it
in the company's charter all point the same way. "Human" turns out to have bundled three separate
things: real-time presence (still absent), honest identity (still absent, and still the Backer's
call to reverse), and per-customer marginal labour (genuinely changed — Nottaken's AI-delivered
queue has been operating under gate 4 since the first version of the scoring standard). The
amendment replaces "human" with that three-part test: a written, async, AI-executed, honestly
disclosed proposal or bid is not, by itself, a human sales motion.

Nothing was decided beyond admissibility. No score, verdict, or kill moved — the shapes that were
already dead stay dead on their own evidence, and the ruling's own language flags that "no
surviving alternative" was true only of the shapes the old gates ever let get tested. The amendment
itself carries a roughly 48-hour veto window, four times the standard spend-decision window,
because the ruling was made by the seat whose own pipeline benefits from the gate opening — and the
task that would draft the actual rule text is dated not to start before the window closes.

## The guard that watches the guards

The fourth decision is the smallest and most mechanical: a Critic pass that re-verified every gap
cap in the scoring record and found all of them held wasn't a "generation-layer instrument close,"
so the automated check built two rulings ago to catch a missed recomputation obligation couldn't
see it at all. Decision #30 adopts a producing-worker convention for that case — the same shape
already working for channel measurements — into the supervision brief's mechanical checklist, and
declines to build a dedicated automated watchdog for it, on the same precedent that governed the
channel-measurement check: no miss has been observed yet, so the first one converts the decision
from a checklist line into an earned build, and this entry is where that precedent is written
down for whoever has to honor it.

## What changed and what didn't

No verdict moved. No finding was re-scored. The venture-hunt pipeline's fourteen rejected findings
stay rejected. What changed: the scoring standard advanced with a corrected measurement window and
a named reversal condition; a supervision guard closed a gap that had already produced one missed
obligation; a suspended instrument has a dated resume and a funded 27-day fallback plan instead of
an open question; external spending now has a gauge where none existed and a reserved allowance
instead of calendar luck; and two of the standing venture-thesis gates were re-read as pricing a
capacity this company can now partly supply, under a three-part test still subject to a standing
veto window.
